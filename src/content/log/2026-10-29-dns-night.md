The same night my first site went live, I tried to make it live at my own address. The site was fine. Getting the internet to agree on where it lived was the hard part.

- **DNS records** are like a phone book for the internet. Each one tells browsers where a name like sutton.pfinn.org should go. Vercel gives you the value, and Namecheap is where you type it in.
- **SSL certificates** are what make the padlock show up. Vercel makes one for you automatically, but only after the DNS record is right, and it takes a few minutes.
- **Analytics** counts visits. Vercel’s version only starts working after a fresh deploy made after you turn it on.

## Things that tripped me up

- Namecheap doesn’t save a record until you click the little green checkmark. I typed a perfect record and then walked away from it.
- “Invalid Configuration” in Vercel almost always meant my DNS record was missing or wrong.
- “Not Secure” right after connecting a domain just meant the certificate wasn’t ready yet. Waiting and refreshing fixed it.
- My Mac and my home router both remembered the old “this site doesn’t exist” answer for hours. The site worked on my phone’s cellular data the whole time. Restarting the router finally cleared it.
- My ad blocker hid my own visits from analytics, which made me think it was broken.
